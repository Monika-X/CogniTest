Introduction

When I first started preparing for IBPS PO, I was working a full-time job and had exactly 90 days until the exam. I had no offline coaching, no study group, and no mentor. What I did have was CogniTest, a structured mindset, and 4 hours every evening.

The result? AIR 1 in IBPS PO 2024. Score: 94.5/100. Selected as Probationary Officer at Punjab National Bank, Chandigarh.

This article is my complete strategy — exactly what I did, day by day, section by section. If I can do it, so can you.

Phase 1 — Weeks 1-4: Foundation Building

The first month is about building strong conceptual foundations. Avoid mock tests in this phase — focus purely on understanding.

Quantitative Aptitude

Start with the basics: Number System, Percentage, Ratio & Proportion, SI & CI. Spend 2 hours daily on theory and 1 hour on practice. Use CogniTest's concept videos by Dr. Arjun Mehta — his shortcut methods for number series saved me 30 seconds per question in the real exam.

"Don't practice problems you can't solve. Understand the concept first, then practice relentlessly."

Logical Reasoning

Focus on Seating Arrangements, Puzzles, and Syllogisms in week 1. These three topics alone contribute 20-22 marks in IBPS PO Prelims.

Phase 2 — Weeks 5-8: Intensive Practice

Now shift to rigorous practice mode. Take 2 subject-wise tests daily, analyze every wrong answer, and maintain an error log.

My error log was a simple notebook where I wrote down every wrong answer with: (1) why I got it wrong, (2) the correct approach, (3) a similar practice question. Reviewing this log every Sunday was one of the most powerful habits of my preparation.

Phase 3 — Weeks 9-12: Refinement & Speed

By Week 9, your foundation should be solid. Now it's about speed, accuracy, and exam strategy.

Take a mock test every alternate day. Track your section-wise timing. In IBPS PO Prelims, you get 20 minutes per section. My optimal strategy was: English (18 min) → Reasoning (18 min) → Quant (24 min).

Exam Day Strategy

Sleep well the night before. Avoid new topics. Reach the center 45 minutes early. Read all questions of each section in the first 2 minutes before attempting.

The most important advice: Don't attempt difficult questions first. Pick your easiest topics, score maximum marks there, then tackle the challenging ones with remaining time.
